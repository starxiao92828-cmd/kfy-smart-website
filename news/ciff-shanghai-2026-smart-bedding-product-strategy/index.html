From CIFF Shanghai 2026 to Customer Conversations: Rethinking Smart Bedding for Global Markets

Insights from CIFF Shanghai and conversations with international partners on building reliable, configurable smart bedding platforms for global B2B programs.

By KFY SMART2026-09-10

KFY SMART team with international customers in Shanghai following product and market discussions during CIFF Shanghai 2026.

Industry exhibitions are useful for discovering new products and technologies. But sometimes, the most valuable product discussions begin after leaving the exhibition hall.

This September, the KFY SMART team visited CIFF Shanghai 2026, with particular attention to sleep furniture, smart bedding and product ideas relevant to international B2B markets.

The 58th China International Furniture Fair (Shanghai) was held from September 5–8, 2026, at the National Exhibition and Convention Center in Shanghai. Hall 3 included New Retail, Smart Sleep, Lifestyle Aesthetics and Materials-related content, while CIFF presented smart sleep as part of a broader integration of products, materials and technology.

For us, however, the most interesting question was not simply: What new functions are appearing in smart beds?

A more useful question is: How should smart bedding products be configured for different brands, markets and customer groups?

From the Exhibition Floor to a Customer Conversation

After visiting CIFF Shanghai, we had dinner with international clients at a Chinese restaurant near the Bund. The conversation moved naturally from what we had seen at the exhibition to smart bedding products, customer expectations and product positioning in different markets.

After dinner, we invited our guests to walk along the Bund. In a more relaxed setting, the discussion continued—from products and sourcing to market preferences and long-term cooperation.

One particular product-development idea stayed with us: Does every smart bed need to be developed as a completely fixed model? Or could a more flexible approach make more sense?

Start with a Reliable Base Version

One idea we discussed was to begin with a reliable base version of a smart bedding product rather than trying to place every possible function into every model. The base product should first solve the fundamentals.

Depending on the product architecture, these may include:

  • reliable adjustment
  • stable operation
  • appropriate mattress and structural design
  • control architecture
  • product compatibility
  • safety and durability
  • consistent manufacturing quality

Once the core product is established, different functions can then be configured according to the target market, brand positioning and price level.

For example, selected configurations may include zero-gravity presets, rocking or cradle-style modes, massage, sleep monitoring, different remote or app control options, lighting or charging functions, and other customized comfort features.

The point is not to add as many functions as possible. The goal is to identify the right combination of functions for the right customer.

Standard, Configurable and Customized

1. Standardize What Determines Reliability

Some elements should remain relatively consistent because they determine whether the product works reliably as a system. These include the core product architecture, adjustment mechanism, structural stability, control logic and basic user experience. For B2B buyers, these fundamentals should come before additional features.

2. Configure What Determines Market Positioning

Different markets do not necessarily need the same feature package. A simpler configuration may be appropriate for an entry-level product, while a higher-positioned model may add functions such as zero gravity, rocking, massage or sleep monitoring where supported. This allows one core product concept to support several market positions without unnecessarily creating completely unrelated products.

3. Customize What Creates Brand Differentiation

Private-label customers may also require differences in product dimensions, fabrics and colors, control interfaces, function combinations, packaging, branding, accessories and other project-specific requirements.

Product Strategy

“Standardize what determines reliability.
Configure what determines market positioning.
Customize what creates brand differentiation.”

Not Every Smart Bed Moves in the Same Way

Another important point from our product discussions is that the term “smart bed” can describe very different product architectures. This matters when buyers compare products.

Adjustable Bed Base-Driven Systems

In a conventional adjustable bed base system, the motor and actuator are installed within the adjustable base. The base changes position and supports the mattress as it moves. For this type of product, the mechanical structure of the base is a major part of the system.

Mattress-Integrated Motion Systems

Another product architecture integrates the motion system directly into the mattress system. In this case, the external standard bed frame can remain stationary while the mattress itself performs the adjustment. This creates a different product concept.

For example, in a dual-zone smart mattress configuration, one side may remain flat while the other side adjusts independently according to the user’s preference. The buyer is therefore not simply comparing two different feature lists; they may actually be comparing two different product architectures. That distinction can influence product design, installation, compatibility, packaging and final market positioning.

From “More Features” to the Right Feature Package

The smart sleep category naturally attracts attention through technology. But technology alone does not automatically create a better commercial product. A model with ten functions is not necessarily more suitable for a market than a model with five carefully selected functions.

For a mattress brand, furniture company or distributor, better questions may be:

  • Which functions will customers actually understand and use?
  • Which features support the intended retail positioning?
  • Which functions should be standard and which should remain optional?
  • Can the same core product serve several market segments?
  • How much differentiation does a private-label customer really need?

This changes product development from How many features can we add? to What is the right product configuration for this market?

One Platform Can Support Different Product Strategies

A configurable approach can make product planning more flexible. Imagine a smart bedding platform with a reliable core structure.

Essential Configuration
Core adjustment and control functions.

Comfort Configuration
Selected comfort presets or additional control options.

Advanced Configuration
Additional comfort or smart features according to the product platform.

The exact naming and configuration will vary by brand and market. But the principle remains: One core product platform does not have to mean one fixed product. It can become a foundation for multiple product programs.

For OEM and private-label projects, this creates opportunities to balance product differentiation, development complexity and market positioning rather than treating every customer request as a completely new product.

Product Architecture Comes Before Marketing Claims

This is why buyers should understand the physical architecture of a smart bedding product before focusing on marketing terminology. Terms such as Smart Bed, Smart Mattress, Adjustable Bed, Zero Gravity and Sleep Monitoring can describe very different products.

The more practical questions are:

  • Where is the motion system located?
  • What actually moves?
  • Can both sides operate independently?
  • Which features are standard?
  • Which are optional?
  • What can realistically be customized?

For global B2B buyers, these answers are often more useful than a long feature list.

Business Is About Products — and People

Our conversation in Shanghai was also a reminder of another part of international business that is easy to overlook. Specifications matter. Quality matters. Pricing matters. But business relationships are still built between people.

The exhibition gave us a reason to meet. Dinner gave us time to discuss products and markets in more detail. And the walk along the Bund created an opportunity to continue the conversation beyond formal business topics.

Those moments help both sides better understand how the other thinks about products, markets and cooperation. For KFY SMART, customer conversations like these are valuable because they help us look at smart bedding from the buyer’s perspective—not only from the supplier’s perspective.

How KFY SMART Approaches Smart Bedding Projects

KFY SMART works with mattress companies, furniture brands, distributors, retailers and project-based buyers on smart sleep product and sourcing solutions. Our product categories include adjustable bed bases, smart beds and smart mattresses.

But our B2B approach is not simply about offering a catalogue of finished models. Different customers may need different product structures, configurations and levels of customization.

Product selection should begin with questions such as:

  • Who is the target customer?
  • Which market will the product enter?
  • What retail positioning is required?
  • Which functions create real value in that market?
  • Which elements need to be customized for the brand?

Only after answering these questions does it make sense to determine the final product configuration.

Building Smarter Products for Different Markets

One of our biggest takeaways from CIFF Shanghai and the conversations that followed was simple: Smart bedding development should not be a competition to add the most functions.

A better product strategy may be to build a reliable foundation first, then select the functions that make sense for each customer and market.
