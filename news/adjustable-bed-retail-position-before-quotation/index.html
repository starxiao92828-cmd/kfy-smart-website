Why Adjustable Bed Buyers Should Define the Retail Position Before Asking for a Quotation

Define the market role before the quotation: a practical B2B framework for positioning an adjustable bed program.

By Willis2026-09-28

For many adjustable bed projects, the first conversation starts with a familiar question: “What is your price?” But for B2B buyers, this is often too early. Two adjustable bed bases may look similar in a quotation sheet while being designed for completely different markets, retail price levels, sales channels and customer expectations.

Before comparing prices, mattress brands, furniture retailers and distributors should first define one thing clearly: “What commercial position is this product supposed to occupy?”

Quick Answer

An adjustable bed quotation only becomes meaningful when the supplier understands the target market, customer segment, intended retail position, required functions, packaging expectations and expected order volume.

Instead of asking suppliers to quote every available configuration, B2B buyers can usually save time by defining the commercial role of the product first.

Market Position → Product Structure → Required Functions → Packaging & Logistics → Target Cost → Quotation

This makes supplier comparisons more accurate and reduces repeated quotation revisions later in the project.

Why Price-First Sourcing Creates Problems

Adjustable bed bases are highly configurable products. Depending on the project, differences may include:

  • steel-frame structure
  • slatted or upholstered platform design
  • foldable or non-foldable construction
  • head and foot articulation
  • massage systems
  • lumbar adjustment
  • zero-clearance capability
  • USB or Type-C charging
  • under-bed lighting
  • wireless or wired remote control
  • app or Bluetooth control
  • wall-saving or sliding movement
  • motor and control-system selection
  • upholstery material
  • packaging structure
  • private-label requirements

If a buyer simply asks several suppliers for their “best price,” each factory may quote a different configuration. The prices are then technically comparable—but commercially meaningless.

Start With the Product's Role in the Market

Before requesting a quotation, buyers should first determine where the adjustable base will sit in their assortment.

Entry-Level Program

The priority may be:

  • essential head and foot adjustment
  • reliable basic structure
  • simple remote control
  • competitive landed cost
  • efficient packaging
  • easy installation

The goal is usually not to maximize features. It is to provide a commercially accessible adjustable-bed solution.

Mainstream Program

A mainstream product normally needs a clearer balance between cost and perceived value. Buyers may consider:

  • stronger or more finished frame construction
  • preset positions
  • lighting
  • charging functions
  • improved upholstery
  • zero-clearance compatibility
  • upgraded remote-control experience

For many mattress and furniture brands, this can become the core volume model.

Premium Program

Premium positioning requires more than simply adding several electronic functions. The complete experience may include:

  • upgraded motion architecture
  • massage
  • lumbar adjustment
  • sliding or wall-saving movement
  • premium upholstery
  • smart controls
  • stronger furniture integration
  • differentiated comfort functions

The supplier should understand why the customer will pay more—not only what functions are technically available.

Target Market Changes the Product

The same adjustable bed base does not necessarily fit every country or sales channel. For example, buyers may have different priorities depending on whether the product is intended for:

Mattress Retail Stores

Showrooms can demonstrate movement, massage and comfort differences directly. Visible and experiential features may therefore have greater commercial value.

E-Commerce

Online buyers cannot physically test the product before purchase. Packaging, installation simplicity, product comparison, shipping efficiency and clear differentiation may become more important.

Furniture Retail

The appearance of the base and its integration with bed frames or bedroom furniture may carry greater weight.

Hospitality or Project Business

Reliability, standardized specifications, maintenance and project installation requirements may matter more than consumer-oriented feature density.

This is why the same supplier may recommend different structures for different projects.

A useful RFQ should help the supplier understand the business behind the product. At minimum, buyers should consider providing:

Target Price Is Useful—But Only With Context

Some buyers hesitate to share a target price because they worry the supplier will simply quote up to that number. That concern is understandable.

However, a target range can be useful when it is linked to a defined specification and market position.

For example: “We are developing a mainstream adjustable base for mattress retail in the UK. The product should include head and foot adjustment, wireless remote, two memory positions and under-bed lighting. Foldable packaging is preferred. Estimated first order is 200–300 units.”

This gives the supplier enough context to evaluate structure, functions and commercial feasibility. It is much more productive than requesting the lowest possible price for an undefined product.

Compare Quotations on the Same Basis

Once the commercial brief is clear, buyers should make sure supplier quotations cover the same specification. Important comparison points may include:

  • frame construction
  • steel specification
  • motor quantity and brand
  • control system
  • weight capacity
  • upholstery
  • included functions
  • accessories
  • packaging
  • certification
  • warranty
  • spare-parts support
  • MOQ
  • lead time
  • payment terms

A lower quoted price may simply reflect a different BOM. This is one of the most common reasons why adjustable-bed quotations appear significantly different between suppliers.

Do Not Over-Specify Too Early

The opposite problem also occurs. Some buyers define every possible feature before understanding whether those functions create meaningful value in their market.

This can lead to:

  • unnecessary cost
  • overly complicated remotes
  • more components
  • additional after-sales scenarios
  • higher spare-parts requirements
  • more difficult sales explanations

For a new project, it can be more effective to define: Must Have → Should Have → Optional. This gives both buyer and supplier room to optimize the product.

FAQ

What information should I provide before requesting an adjustable bed quotation?

At minimum, provide the target market, required sizes, estimated quantity, key functions, packaging requirements, certification needs and branding requirements. If possible, also explain the intended customer segment and retail position.

Should I send suppliers a target price?

A target price range can be useful when the expected specification and market positioning are already reasonably clear. Without that context, the number alone provides limited value.

Why are quotations from different adjustable bed suppliers sometimes very different?

Different suppliers may be quoting different frame structures, motors, control systems, steel specifications, functions, upholstery, packaging or warranty conditions. The quotations should be compared using the same technical and commercial baseline.

Should buyers request every available function?

Not necessarily. The best configuration depends on the target market and customer. More functions can increase cost and complexity without necessarily improving commercial performance.

Conclusion

For adjustable bed sourcing, the most useful question is often not: “What is your lowest price?” It is: “What product are we actually trying to build?”

Once the market position, customer segment and sales channel are clear, suppliers can make better recommendations, buyers can compare quotations more accurately, and projects usually move faster. A clear commercial brief at the beginning can prevent repeated specification changes later.
